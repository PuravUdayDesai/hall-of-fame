The brief

Strong company:Shrinking front door

Adobe makes more money than ever. Fewer new creators start there. We studied the company in three passes: its financial health, its stock, and its market. All of it served one question: what should Adobe do in the next 12 months so it still leads in five years?

Context
Generative AI collapsed the cost of making things. Canva, Figma and a wave of AI-native tools now compete for the same creators Adobe trained for 40 years.
Problem
Strength without reach. Adobe dominates enterprise, but only 14% of student and Gen Z creative tool use happens in Creative Cloud.
Approach
Read the financials, test them against the stock’s fall, map the market forces, list Adobe’s internal tensions, then weigh three strategic alternatives against them.
Recommendation
Stand up an Agile Studio now, ship a simplified mobile freemium tier within 12 months, and grow into the most trusted creative AI platform in 3–5 years.

Inside the numbers:A financial fortress

Before asking whether Adobe is losing, we checked whether it can afford to fight. The answer is yes, comfortably.

The $1B Figma termination fee

Adobe announced plans to acquire Figma in September 2022. A year later the deal was called off after U.S. and European antitrust regulators objected. The fee dented profit for a year and left cash flow intact.

R&D grew 13.56% over 2023

Margins held even with heavy R&D and marketing spend. Growth has moved from acquisitions (Magento and Marketo added hundreds of millions in recurring revenue) to organic AI products like Firefly.

Balance sheet: Expanding assets, disciplined liabilities

Total assets grew 24% from 2020 to 2024 while revenue grew 67%. Adobe is producing more with roughly the same base.

  • Falling equity is a choice

    Adobe reduced equity through buybacks, trading short-term balance sheet strength for more value per share. Consistent profits and cash flow make that sustainable.

  • Higher liabilities fund innovation

    In 2024, liabilities rose mainly from new loans and short-term debt nearing maturity. That kept more cash free for AI development and Creative Cloud upgrades.

  • Flat assets, rising revenue

    Assets barely moved in 2021–22 and again in 2023–24 while revenue kept climbing. Growth no longer needs more capital.

Market position: Still the default for professionals

Price action: Four sell-offs, four different fears

Each drop came with its own story. Read together, they show the market’s concern moving from AI monetization to competition and then to pricing.

  1. Dec ’24−14.0%

    Cool tech, but no profit

    AI features shipped faster than AI revenue. Investors wanted proof that Firefly pays for itself.

  2. Mar ’25−11.0%

    Competitive panic

    Canva, Figma and AI-native tools all launched features aimed directly at Adobe users.

  3. Jun ’25−6.0%

    Fewer subscriptions

    Concern that new subscriber growth was slowing, especially among individual creators.

  4. Sep ’25−4.5%

    Cheaper alternatives

    Free and low-cost tools kept pressure on Adobe’s premium pricing.

Each sell-off was smaller than the last. The market was adjusting its expectations, not panicking, and the fears it named line up with the market forces in the next chapter.

Bull vs. bear: Why we still rate Adobe an attractive investment

We weighed what the price ignores against what it is worried about.

Bull case

Strong growth & profitability

11%+ revenue growth in 2025, with a 31% net profit margin and an 89% gross margin.

AI integration leadership

Firefly and Acrobat AI Assistant are driving ARR growth in both digital media and customer experience.

Competitive moat

$18.6B Digital Media ARR, 74% of revenue. The product ecosystem and high switching costs keep customers loyal.

Bear case

Losing the next generation

Only 14% of student and Gen Z creative tool use happens in Creative Cloud. Canva has 81.2%.

Pricing pressure

Free and cheap AI-native tools make a premium subscription harder to justify for new users.

Regulatory and balance sheet weight

Three AI regulation cases, plus goodwill and intangibles at about 45% of total assets.

Valuation appeal

About 15× forward earnings with a PEG under 1.0. Buffett-style intrinsic value models put fair value above $583 a share, and 28 Wall Street analysts hold a Moderate Buy consensus with roughly 37% upside.

Key investment criteria

Today’s market rewards sustainable growth, strong free cash flow, AI-driven innovation and durable competitive advantages. Adobe meets all four on current numbers.

Investment thesis: Adobe combines financial strength, innovation leadership and an attractive valuation, which makes it a high-quality long-term holding. The discount exists because the market doubts Adobe’s reach with the next generation. Our strategy is built to close that gap.

Inside Adobe: Strong foundation, slowing agility

Every strength we found in the financials has a matching weakness in the product.

Enterprise power, Gen Z gap

Adobe dominates enterprise, but younger creators choose simpler tools.

Pricing up, clarity down

A high-margin subscription model with confusing tiers for new users.

Strong brand, heavy product

Trust is high, but the tools feel bloated next to AI-native rivals.

Reliable revenue, slower innovation

A stable business whose agility is fading.

Stable but stalled: The market doubles, Adobe’s friction stays

Digital content creation will more than double by 2030. The growth is there for whoever makes it easiest to start.

But Adobe is still struggling with:

Subscription price sentiment

Users feel they pay a lot for tools they only partly use.

A complex customer journey

Too many apps, plans and tiers to choose between.

Onboarding friction

The first hour in Adobe is harder than the first hour in Canva.

Problem statement

Adobe owns the professional creative market but is losing first contact with the next generation to faster, cheaper, AI-native tools, in a market set to double by 2030.

4Decide · Strategic alternatives

Three bets:Rebuild, open up, or monetize the community

We tested each alternative against the problem: does it reach new creators, how fast, and what does it put at risk?

Alternative 1

The AI-native creative environment

From a collection of apps to the OS of global creativity

The idea
Replace a suite of separate apps with one AI-first workspace where every tool and model is a step away.
Solves
“Heavy product” and the complex customer journey.
Risk
A multi-year rebuild. It does little for Gen Z reach in the next 12 months.
Became the 3–5 year vision
Alternative 2

Creative Cloud Free

Invite the world into Adobe

The idea
A free entry point to Creative Cloud so the first design a student makes is made in Adobe.
Solves
The Gen Z gap, price sentiment and onboarding friction.
Risk
Free can cannibalize paid plans unless the upgrade path is designed in.
Refined into the 12-month priority
Alternative 3

Firefly Marketplace

A new creative economy

The idea
A marketplace where creators sell assets, styles and templates made with commercially safe Firefly models.
Solves
Creator trust and a new revenue stream on top of subscriptions.
Risk
A marketplace needs a large creator base first, and adds licensing exposure.
Held until reach is rebuilt

Empower every creator, everywhere:Adobe’s path to creative AI leadership

Vision: become the world’s most trusted and accessible creative AI platform within 3–5 years. The roadmap has three tiers, each unlocking the next.

Immediate · 60–90 day cycles

Immediate action

Adobe Agile Studio: a cross-functional taskforce built to ship fast.

  • Mobile-first workflow development
  • Content Credentials on by default

12-month priority

Simplified mobile tier: freemium acquisition for mobile-first creators.

  • Express, Lightroom Mobile and Firefly tools
  • Canva-level ease with Adobe power
  • AI-powered quick actions built in
3–5 years

3–5 year vision

Trusted AI leader: ethical standards through independent governance.

  • Creative Cloud and Experience Cloud unified
  • AI-native workflows across all devices
  • Global accessibility at every level

The single most important action

Next 12 months: Release a simplified, low-cost tier built for mobile and AI-first creation

Mobile-first generation

Capture younger creators before competitors do.

AI feature velocity

Ship Firefly innovations faster with agile cycles.

Upgrade economics

A freemium entry that still leads to premium plans.

Lead, if it moves first.
